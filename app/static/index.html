The pipeline

1 · ModelGemma 4 12B (4-bit) with a LoRA adapter reads the numbered transcript and writes one JSON object: identity, chief complaint, assessment / response / education bullets, each with a verbatim quote and cited turns, typed facts, risk flags.
2 · Rules16 deterministic checks against the transcript: quotes really in the cited turns, numbers, drugs and names spoken, planned vs completed, negatives, hedges, Not-Applicable gate, risk flags. Near-miss quotes are repaired.
3 · RenderA deterministic renderer turns the JSON into the exact reference text.
4 · JudgeThe base model with frozen prompts checks faithfulness, completeness and calibration (live mode, optional).
5 · GateA rule error that survives repair, a drug the caller said that the summary lacks (medication safety net), or invalid JSON, becomes NEEDS REVIEW or FAILED for a nurse, never a final summary.

The LLM judge was validated before it was used

We deliberately damaged 90 summaries with known defects (a wrong number, a swapped drug, an invented finding, a flipped negative, a removed item, planned → completed, a swapped speaker, a removed hedge). 50 were used to tune the judge prompts; the prompts were then frozen and run once on 40 fresh ones.

What the PASS / REVIEW / FAILED banner means

PASS: no rule error survives the automatic quote repair. It is not a correctness guarantee. The 16 rules cannot judge whether a drug or dose is missing (rule V15 only warns, and warnings do not stop a PASS), so a separate medication safety net compares the drugs the caller said with the summary: a drug the caller said with a number, or twice, that the summary never mentions turns the call into NEEDS NURSE REVIEW (va-039: lisinopril). The net catches both real omissions in the validation calls but also raises false alarms (2 of its 9 flags were real), so a flag means "please check", not "wrong". NEEDS REVIEW also means a rule error remains. FAILED: the output is not valid JSON. Every summary still needs a nurse.

Research prototype on synthetic data. Not a medical device; every summary must be reviewed by a clinician.
